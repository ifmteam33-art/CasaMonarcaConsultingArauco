Who is Arauco México?

Celulosa Arauco y Constitución S.A. operates wood panel plants in Durango and Zitácuaro. Its revenues are denominated in MXN, while key inputs — urea and methanol — are quoted and paid in USD.

📊

The Risk

A depreciation of the MXN directly increases production costs. When the dollar strengthens, operating margins compress with no short-term ability to pass the cost increase on to the market.

🔄

The Solution

A Cross-Currency Swap locks in today the rate at which Arauco will deliver MXN and receive USD over 6 months, eliminating FX uncertainty across its raw material purchase cycle.

Executive Summary

Casa Monarca Consulting recommends implementing a MXN/USD Currency Swap with a notional of USD 2.0 million — representing Arauco México's residual unhedged chemical exposure after accounting for existing natural hedges (USD export revenues, in-house resin production, and local wood fiber sourcing). The swap matures in November 2026, covering the USMCA review window in July 2026, and locks in a rate of 18.10 MXN/USD on the unhedged portion of urea and methanol purchases.

Arauco México's Existing Natural Hedges

Before designing any hedging instrument, it is essential to recognize that Arauco México already carries partial internal offsets that reduce its net FX exposure. The swap targets only the residual gap these hedges do not cover.

🇺🇸

USD Export Revenues

Arauco ships wood panels from its Mexican plants to U.S. customers, generating USD inflows that naturally offset a portion of USD-denominated input costs at the subsidiary level.

🌲

Local Wood Fiber

Raw wood fiber is sourced domestically in Durango — a major pine-producing state. This eliminates a significant USD-denominated input, reducing overall FX sensitivity.

🏭

In-House Resin Plant

The Durango facility produces resins and formalin locally, reducing reliance on USD-priced chemical imports. Only specialty chemicals (urea, methanol) remain fully exposed.

👷

MXN Labor & Utilities

Labor, electricity, and facility leases are contracted in pesos. When MXN depreciates, these costs fall in USD terms — partially offsetting higher peso costs of imported inputs.

Why a Currency Swap — and Why Not the Alternatives

Arauco México has predictable, recurring USD cash outflows for urea and methanol — exactly the use case for which a currency swap was designed. The instrument is OTC (not a forward or a future), carries zero upfront premium, and mirrors the structure covered in class: a Mexican entity pays a fixed MXN rate (TIIE-based) and receives a fixed USD rate (SOFR-based) on a notional amount, exchanging cash flows periodically. Straddles and strangles were ruled out because they profit from volatility in both directions — Arauco is not speculating, it is hedging a cost. Plain options would require paying a premium, adding cost without improving the protection profile for a recurring, predictable exposure. The swap is the cleanest fit.
