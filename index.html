Risk Analysis
What Could Go Wrong?

The main risks Arauco faces beyond day-to-day economic fluctuations.

High

Legal Uncertainty

Mexico changed how judges are elected, weakening legal protections for businesses. Moody's and Fitch warned this could damage foreign investment. For Arauco: harder to enforce contracts and protect their $290M USD investment.

High

Energy Problems

Mexico's electricity grid (CFE) has not expanded fast enough for new industrial demand. Arauco's plants require significant electricity. Outages could halt production and raise costs significantly.

Medium-High

Security & Crime

Organized crime controls some transport routes. Companies allocate 2–10% of their budgets to security. Arauco's weekly shipments from Durango to North American plants face supply chain risk.

Critical Global Risk

The 2026 Iran War

In February 2026, military operations blocked 20% of global oil supply. Oil jumped from $73 to over $110 per barrel. For Arauco: higher energy costs, more expensive chemical inputs, and weaker U.S. housing demand.

Critical

USMCA Trade Review

The North American trade agreement goes under formal review in July 2026. Arauco ships products between Mexico and the U.S. every week under this deal. Rule changes could severely disrupt their supply chain.

Opportunity

Nearshoring & Sustainability

Arauco holds FSC sustainability certification — increasingly required by U.S. and European retailers. Mexico's growing AI data center build-out creates new demand for Arauco's industrial panels.

Part 2 — The Strategy
The Zero-Cost Collar

What is a Collar and Why Use It?

A Zero-Cost Collar combines two options into one strategy with no upfront cost:

Buy a USD Call Option — Cap (max Arauco pays)Strike: 17.56 MXN/USD
Sell a USD Put Option — Floor (give up extreme gains)Strike: 16.79 MXN/USD
Net Premium$0 — Zero Cost
Why is this right for Arauco? They buy USD regularly and need protection if the peso weakens significantly. The collar caps their worst case at 17.56 — acceptable and predictable for budget planning. The two option premiums cancel each other out, so there is no upfront payment.

When the Collar Helps

When USD/MXN rises above 17.56 — the peso weakens beyond the base case. Arauco's costs are capped. In the worst case (19.23), they save over $19 million MXN vs. being unhedged.

When the Collar Has a Cost

When USD/MXN falls below 16.79 — peso strengthens significantly. Arauco misses cheaper imports and pays more than spot. Maximum opportunity cost: ~$9.2M MXN if rate hits 16.00.

Limitations

The collar manages risk but doesn't generate profit. It requires a bank counterparty to trade both legs. If Arauco's actual volumes differ from the $11.7M estimate, the hedge ratio needs adjustment.

Executive Brief
Final Recommendation
01

Implement the Zero-Cost Collar Now

With USD/MXN at 17.31 and multiple risk factors pointing to peso weakness — USMCA review, Iran war, compressing rate differential — Arauco should lock in the 17.56 cap immediately. Zero upfront cost.

02

6-Month Horizon is the Right Choice

Arauco's purchases are monthly and continuous. A 6-month hedge covers the most critical period — through the USMCA review in July 2026 — when volatility is highest.

03

Reassess After the USMCA Outcome

After July 2026, re-evaluate the strategy. A positive USMCA result may allow a simpler forward. A negative result may require rolling the collar at a higher strike.
